    Case study · Café Pacundí

    From the land to the cup: the Café Pacundí redesign

    A single origin coffee from Pacho, Cundinamarca, with a site frozen in 2019. We brought it up to date, showed everything Pacundí is, joined digital and in store buying, and gave a voice to its social side.

    Case study · Dafiti · Advanced UX/UI Design

    Less wandering, more fashion: a UX/UI redesign of Dafiti

    The largest online fashion platform in Latin America had confusing filters, unclear sizes and a cart that raised doubts. I listened to 22 shoppers, tested the navigation and turned the findings into a prototype validated with users.

    On this page
    UX/UI · Fashion retail · 2025

    Overview

    I redesigned the Dafiti web shopping experience end to end: research with real users, information architecture, its own design system and a high fidelity prototype in Figma that went through two versions thanks to usability testing.

    Academic project Developed in the Advanced UX/UI Design course at CoderHouse. It wasn't commissioned by Dafiti.

    Context
    Advanced UX/UI Design · CoderHouse
    My role
    Product Designer UX/UI
    Timeline
    August to November 2025
    Deliverable

    What Dafiti is

    An online store for fashion, footwear and accessories for men, women and kids, with search filters, several payment methods, shipping and returns. Its experience is built to turn visits into sales.

    • 100.000active products
    • 700partner brands
    • AutoStorethe largest in the world
    • Distribution centerlargest fashion platform in Latin America
    01 · The starting point

    A huge catalog, hard to get around

    Three pain points came up in user testing. All three stop the purchase right when the person already wants to buy.

    Clarity when buyingPeople need to confirm their actions, like removing a product or completing the purchase, so they aren't left with doubts.
    Faster navigationFilters and search aren't efficient, so finding a product takes longer than it should.
    Less cart abandonmentA more visible and simpler checkout helps people finish the purchase without friction.
    The problem, in one sentence

    Searching, filtering and reviewing the cart is confusing. That leads people to abandon products and lowers conversion, especially among young users who shop through digital channels.

    02 · Research

    Three methods, one question

    Where do people get lost when buying fashion online? I answered it by comparing competitors, surveying shoppers and measuring navigation with tree testing.

    Desk research: how competitors solve it

    CriterionZaraÉxito ModaMercado Libre

    Ease of navigationLowNo clear guidance, confusing menusMediumLong but accessible menuHighSimplified navigation, menus by category
    Filters and searchLimited or hard to seePresent, but not very efficientAutomatic, visible and combinable
    Product informationImage focused, few technical detailsAdequate, with clear physical representationsRich descriptions, size guides and reviews

    What I took away: Mercado Libre set the benchmark with automatic filters, complete product information and visible confirmations.

    If

    Case study · UMNG Anatomy Museum

    A museum that existed, but nobody knew about.

    I led a team of eight people to turn a collection of real anatomical specimens, kept in an isolated room on the Cajicá campus, into a digital experience anyone can explore from their computer or phone.

    On this page
    UX/UI · AR, VR and 3D · 2024

    Overview

    The Universidad Militar Nueva Granada holds a collection of real anatomical specimens with high scientific and educational value. The problem was that they lived in formaldehyde, in an isolated room on the Cajicá campus, and most of the university community didn't even know the museum existed.

    The Museums Division gave us a concrete challenge: give that collection visibility. I led a team of eight people to turn the museum into a digital experience anyone could explore from their computer or phone.

    Academic project with a real client Multimedia Engineering, Multimedia Integration course: 3D Applications.

    Client
    Museums Division, UMNG
    My role
    Project director · UX/UI designer
    Duration
    15 weeks, February to May 2024
    Status
    Prototype delivered, transitioning to production
    Tools
    FigmaUnityNeedle EngineThree.jsReactMaya
    01 · The problem

    The collection didn't lack value, it lacked reach

    Analyzing the situation with the client, we found the museum faced three barriers.

    Limited accessIt was in Cajicá, out of reach for a large part of the community.
    Almost no promotionThe outreach budget was practically zero.
    Static contentThe experience depended entirely on being in the room.
    02 · Who we designed for

    Two profiles, the same barriers

    We defined two archetypes with the team and the client.

    17 to 30 years old

    Students

    Driven by curiosity about the human body.

    35 to 60 years old

    Staff

    With little time available and little familiarity with 3D experiences.

    What they shared

    • Not being able to travel to Cajicá
    • Depending on their device or connection
    • In some cases, aversion to the idea of seeing real cadaveric specimens
    The opportunity

    The digital format lets people get close to the specimens with a distance the physical room doesn't offer.

    03 · Strategy

    Two ways to visit a museum

    After reviewing references like the Heidelberg Anatomy Museum and the Visible Human project, we structured the experience into two complementary paths.

    For those who want to study

    Each specimen is shown on its own, with its interactive 3D model and detailed anatomical information.

    For those who want to live the visit

    A navigable 3D space, like a video game, that recreates the museum room.

    A cyclical narrative of five specimens

    I designed the map of the space: a hexagonal room where each specimen has its own display point and a clear visiting order.

    1. Brain
    2. Skull
    3. Diaphragmatic block
    4. Upper limb
    5. Lower limb
    Diagram of the visiting order.
    04 · Interface design

    The specimen is always the protagonist

    That was the client's main requirement. I built the visual system on UMNG's institutional identity, deep blue with yellow accents, and designed every screen so the interface would get out of the way.

    Home

    A direct entry with a photo of the campus and only two decisions: Virtual Tour or Explorer. From the first second, users choose how they want to experience the museum.

    Gallery

    The exhibition is presented as a carousel of the five specimens, so you can see at a glance what the museum holds before going deeper into one.

    Detail view: a digital display case

    Each specimen floats on a blue halo that isolates it from the white background, as if it were lit inside a display case. Scroll the screen inside the frame or tap each point.

    07 · Motion

    Three speeds and one curve

    All the motion on the site uses three fixed durations and a curve that starts fast and slows down gently. Tap each row to see the difference.

    Ease out curve · .16 · 1 · .3 · 1

    The element arrives almost immediately and settles slowly. It feels responsive without looking abrupt.

    Rules

    • It respects “reduce motion”. If the person asked for it in their system, the custom cursor, the click ripple, the tilt, the magnetism and the entrances turn off; animated things stay still.
    • Mouse only. The crayon, the magnetism and the tilt don't activate on touch screens.
    • Only once. Scroll entrances don't repeat when you scroll back up.
    • It pauses off screen. The halftone footer and long animations don't drain battery when they're not visible.

    The Home page went through three versions before the current one. Each iteration left something that stayed and something that was dropped.

    11 · Learnings

    What this project leaves me

    01Iterate faster

    Going from a design to a working prototype in a short time, generating code and design at once. Every idea was tested in a real browser before deciding whether it stayed.

    02Learn to simplify

    It's easy to add features that aren't what the user needs. The site got better every time something was removed: a page, a filter, form steps, the résumé.

    Many microinteractions don't catch your eye at first, but when the site invites you to play, people draw on the grid, move the cards, open the notebook or send a letter. That's where the personality is.
